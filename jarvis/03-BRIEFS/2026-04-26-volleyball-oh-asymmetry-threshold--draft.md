# The 10% asymmetry rule is wrong for half your volleyball roster.

You bilateral-test your outside hitter. Landing impulse difference: **14%**.

Most clinics call that a flag. Some start return-to-play protocols.

You should ask a different question first.

## Where the 10% rule actually comes from

The **10% asymmetry threshold** comes from rehab literature on symmetric-sport populations. Distance running. Cycling. ACL return-to-play in pivoting sports with bilaterally balanced demand.

Volleyball outside hitters are not in that population.

## Why OH asymmetry is structural

A right-handed OH approaches the attack with a **left-right-left** pattern. She plants asymmetrically. She lands asymmetrically. Block jumps lean one-footed depending on net position. Defensive footwork is side-dominant. Hours of training repeat this pattern.

Over a season an OH's landing-impulse profile *should* be asymmetric. The training is asymmetric. The sport is asymmetric.

Applying a symmetric threshold to an asymmetric job description mislabels healthy adaptation as risk.

## The reversed-methodology problem

Here is what most coaches and clinicians miss.

An OH inside the 10% band may have *lost* the trained sport-specific asymmetry. That is its own flag. The player has gone neutral in a sport that does not reward neutral.

Look at her last three months. Post-concussion protocol. A contralateral injury that quietly changed her plant leg. A conservative programming block after a mild hamstring strain. Each of those drags an OH back toward symmetry.

She is "passing" the test. She is also less prepared for sport demands than she was three months ago.

## A diagnostic that works in 60 seconds

Pull your last 5 OHs' bilateral landing data.

1. Plot each player's **landing-impulse asymmetry** against her sport-side dominance (which leg plants on attack landing).
2. Players asymmetric *in the sport's direction* — that is adaptation. Tag green.
3. Players asymmetric *against* the sport's direction — that is a flag, regardless of magnitude. Tag red.
4. Players neutral (under 6%) on a position that demands asymmetry — that is also a flag. Tag yellow.

Now you have a position-specific risk picture instead of a binary 10% test.

## What this changes about programming

Generic bilateral strength work for an OH with low sport-direction asymmetry will not restore the pattern. The fix is loaded sport-specific approach work, jump landings from approach height, and block-step plyometrics, before any further bilateral squat volume.

The asymmetry is not the enemy. The wrong-direction asymmetry is.
