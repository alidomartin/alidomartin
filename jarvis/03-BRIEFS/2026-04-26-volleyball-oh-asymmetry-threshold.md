# Brief: The 10% asymmetry rule is wrong for volleyball OHs

## ONE THING
The **10% landing-impulse asymmetry threshold** was derived from symmetric-sport rehab populations. Volleyball outside hitters are structurally asymmetric by sport demand, so applying the rule uniformly mislabels healthy adaptation as risk and misses players who have *lost* the sport-specific asymmetry they need.

## PROOF
- The 10% rule traces to rehab literature on bilaterally balanced movement (cycling, distance running, ACL RTP in pivoting sports with symmetric base demands).
- A right-handed OH approaches with an L-R-L pattern, plants asymmetrically, lands asymmetrically. Block jumps and defensive footwork are side-dominant. The training pattern is asymmetric. The sport pattern is asymmetric.
- The mechanical context: clean reference CMJ landing impact peaks ~1800 N above BW within 16 ms of contact. Whatever direction that load enters, it does so within an eyeblink. There is no "average" of left and right that matters here.

## READER TRANSFORMATION
A volleyball coach or sport scientist walks away with a new diagnostic question — "Is my OH's asymmetry in the direction her sport demands?" — instead of a binary pass/fail against a generic threshold. They also walk away with a flag they did not have before: low-asymmetry OHs may be a bigger problem than high-asymmetry ones.

3. Position-specific thresholds. Not roster-wide rules.
