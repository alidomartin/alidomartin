# Brief: The deceleration ceiling on jump height

## ONE THING
Low CMJ jump height is more often a **braking deficit** than a propulsion deficit, and the default fix of loaded triple-extension does nothing when the real limiter is eccentric tolerance.

## PROOF
On a clean reference CMJ, peak braking force lands at ~1.75×BW *before* propulsive force can reach ~2.1×BW. Braking pre-loads propulsion mechanically. Athletes who undershoot the braking peak cannot reach the propulsive peak, regardless of how many Cleans, Trap Bar Jumps, or Pin Squats they do.

## READER TRANSFORMATION
The reader (an S&C coach or sport scientist) walks away able to look at any flat CMJ trace and ask one diagnostic question — "is the braking peak undershooting 1.5×BW?" — before prescribing any concentric work, and knows the eccentric-overload alternative when it is.

1. **Aggressive:** Most coaches treat low jump height as a propulsion problem. The force trace usually says it is a braking problem.

## CLOSERS
1. Pull up your last 10 athletes' force traces. Count how many have a braking peak under 1.5×BW. That is your over-prescribed concentric work, on a list.
2. Triple extension cannot save what triple flexion never loaded.
3. If the braking peak is not there, add it before you add propulsion.
