# mRSI is the bottom-of-V time wearing a different name

**Bridge:** The open question of whether **mRSI** is a sharper daily readiness marker than CMJ jump height collapses once you notice mRSI is encoding the same construct as transfer-point time at the bottom of the CMJ V — both measure how briefly the athlete tolerates the eccentric-to-concentric switch.

**Potential hook:** The "mRSI vs jump height" debate is a category error. They are not measuring the same thing.

## Why this is non-obvious
Jump height is an **outcome metric**. mRSI and transfer-point time are **strategy metrics**. Outcome metrics are stable under fatigue because athletes compensate. Strategy metrics shift earlier because compensation costs time at the bottom of the V. This reframes the original question into something testable in 4 sessions: track transfer-point time across a basketball competitive block and see whether it moves before jump height does on the same athletes.
