# N1 Brand — Concentration Strategy

## The Principle

> "Concentration builds wealth. Diversification preserves it."
> — Warren Buffett / Charlie Munger

This is one of the most important strategic truths in business — and almost every early-stage brand violates it.

**Diversification** makes sense once you have wealth to protect. Spreading risk across many assets prevents any single loss from being catastrophic. But diversification also caps your upside — you cannot outperform if your bets are spread thin.

**Concentration** is how wealth is created in the first place. The biggest fortunes — in investing, in business, in brand-building — come from betting heavily on one clear advantage and riding it to dominance before branching out.

Applied to brand strategy: **you build authority by going narrow and deep, not wide and shallow.**

---

## Why This Matters for N1

N1 exists in the sports performance analytics space. The temptation for any performance brand is to become a "one-stop shop" — training programs, nutrition advice, recovery protocols, mental skills, general fitness content. That path feels safe because it serves more people.

It is actually the most dangerous path for a young brand.

A brand that covers everything is remembered for nothing. A brand that owns one thing is the first call when that thing matters.

N1's one thing is the **CMJ force-time curve** — specifically, the ability to read, interpret, and act on what the curve reveals about an athlete's neuromuscular state, readiness, and movement quality.

This is not a small niche. Force plate testing is the fastest-growing monitoring tool in elite sport. The gap between owning a force plate and knowing what to do with the data is enormous. N1 fills that gap.

---

## What Concentration Looks Like for N1

### 1. Own the CMJ Interpretation Space

Become the definitive reference for CMJ force-time analysis — not one of many voices, but the go-to source. Every piece of content, every tool, every framework N1 produces should deepen its authority on this single topic.

The CMJ phase guide already demonstrates this. The breakdown of Quiet → Unweighting → Braking → Transfer → Propulsive → Flight → Landing, with clear definitions, diagnostic logic, and evidence-based reference points, is exactly the kind of concentrated IP that builds a brand's reputation.

Build more of this. Go deeper, not wider.

### 2. Concentrate Around Specific, Ownable Metrics

N1 should develop a recognizable vocabulary around a small set of high-signal metrics:

| Metric | What It Reveals |
|---|---|
| **mRSI** (modified Reactive Strength Index) | Neuromuscular readiness and efficiency |
| **PRPP** (Peak Relative Propulsive Power) | Peak power output during propulsion |
| **LPI** (Landing Performance Index) | Landing quality and injury risk proxy |
| **Transfer Point Duration** | Amortization efficiency |
| **Braking Impulse** | Eccentric force absorption capacity |

These should become N1's signature language. When a coach hears "mRSI dropped 12% — that's a Transfer Point problem," they should immediately associate that precision with N1.

### 3. The Concentration Flywheel

Concentration compounds. Each piece of deep CMJ content does three things simultaneously:

1. **Attracts the right audience** — S&C coaches, sports scientists, and high-performance staff who actually use force plates and need interpretation frameworks.
2. **Repels the wrong audience** — casual fitness audiences who would dilute the brand's positioning and distract its content.
3. **Builds defensible IP** — frameworks, naming conventions, and diagnostic logic that become associated with N1, not with competitors.

Over time, this depth creates a moat. A brand that has published 50 pieces of expert-level CMJ content is not easy to displace. A brand that has published 10 pieces on CMJ, 10 on nutrition, 10 on sleep, and 10 on sprint mechanics has no moat at all.

### 4. When to Diversify

Diversification is appropriate **after** N1 has established clear authority in the CMJ space. The signal to diversify is not a calendar date — it is when:

- N1 is the first brand cited in CMJ discussions among its target audience
- Coaches and scientists refer inbound traffic to N1 without being asked
- N1's phase terminology and metric definitions are being adopted by others

At that point, adjacent expansion (e.g., isometric mid-thigh pull analysis, sprint force-velocity profiling, or RSI from drop jump) is additive rather than dilutive — because the brand foundation is already solid.

Expanding before that point spreads the brand's limited attention across topics where it has no earned authority, competing against established voices on their home turf.

---

## The Practical Implication

Every content, product, or partnership decision at N1 should pass one filter:

**Does this deepen N1's authority on CMJ force-time analysis, or does it distract from it?**

If it deepens: build it.

If it distracts: defer it until the foundation is unshakeable.

The athletes and coaches who win championships are not the ones who are decent at everything. They are the ones who are exceptional at one thing and have built everything else around that core strength.

N1 is the brand that reads the force-time curve better than anyone. That is the wealth. Protect it by concentrating on it — until it is time to diversify from a position of strength.
