## 00 — The So What

> **Impulse wins games. Peak force is theater.**

The athlete who transfers momentum fastest at the bottom of the "V" — not the one who pushes hardest — dictates play speed, injury resilience, and positional dominance. Every coaching decision this cycle should route back to one question:
**How efficiently is this athlete converting braking impulse into propulsive impulse?**

If the Transfer Point is slow, everything downstream is compromised — regardless of how impressive the peak GRF looks on the leaderboard.

---

## 01 — Framework: The Impulse-Momentum Hierarchy

| Priority | Metric | Why It Leads |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Braking Impulse | Governs COM deceleration efficiency — directly upstream of power output |
| **2** | Propulsive Impulse | Net vertical momentum sent into flight — true output signal |
| **3** | Transfer Point Duration | Amortization speed — the "switch phase" latency between eccentric and concentric |
| **4** | mRSI (modified RSI) | Jump height ÷ contact time — systemic load tolerance proxy |
| **5** | Landing Control Time | Phase 3 efficiency — COM stabilization speed post-impact |
| *Deprioritized* | Peak GRF | Snapshot, not story — high peak force with poor impulse ratio = inefficient athlete |

---

## 02 — Phase-by-Phase Forensic Map

### Quiet Phase
**Signal:** System weight (N) baseline integrity.
**Watch for:** Fidgeting, load shifting, emotional arousal bleed.
**Coaching note:** A contaminated quiet phase corrupts every downstream calculation. Standardize the cue.

---

### Unweighting Phase
**Signal:** Rate and depth of COM drop — voluntary free-fall quality.
**Impulse-momentum lens:** Athletes who "fall" deeper create a longer runway for braking impulse accumulation. Shallow unweighting = compressed braking window = reduced propulsive capacity.
**Position implication:** A **Middle Blocker** or **Power Forward** with a restricted unweighting phase will show a chronically short propulsive window — misread as a strength deficit when it is actually a motor sequencing issue.

---

### Braking Phase
**Signal:** Braking impulse (N·s), peak negative COM velocity.
**Impulse-momentum lens:** This is the load-bearing phase. Braking impulse should be large enough to decelerate COM cleanly but brief enough to preserve elastic energy.
**Red flag:** Excessive braking time with low impulse = poor eccentric rate of force development. Prescribe: Nordic Hamstring Curls, Eccentric Overload, Accentuated Eccentric Loading.

---

### Transfer Point *(Priority Watch)*
**Signal:** Duration at minimum COM displacement — time "at the bottom."
**Impulse-momentum lens:** The single highest-leverage diagnostic in this system. A long transfer point bleeds elastic energy stored during braking. The athlete is, in effect, leaking momentum.
**Strategic insight:** A **Point Guard** or **Setter** with a prolonged Transfer Point will always be a half-beat late on first-step explosiveness — not because they lack strength, but because they cannot recycle stored energy fast enough. Reactive plyometrics > traditional strength work for this deficiency.

---

### Propulsive Phase
**Signal:** Propulsive impulse (N·s), average & peak PRPP (Relative Propulsive Power).
**Impulse-momentum lens:** Outcome of everything above. If braking and transfer are clean, propulsive metrics will reflect true contractile capacity. If not, they are noise.
**Prescription trigger:** Low propulsive impulse despite good braking → Cleans, Trap Bar Jump, Pin Squats. Low propulsive impulse with poor braking → fix upstream first.

---

### Flight Phase
**Signal:** Jump height (calculated from takeoff velocity — not flight time).
**Note:** Flight time is a lagging indicator. Takeoff velocity is the honest signal.

---

### Landing Phase — 3-Phase Breakdown

| Sub-Phase | Signal | Priority Metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Loading** | Initial contact → Peak GRF | Loading Rate, v at contact |
| **Attenuation** | Peak GRF → Local minimum | Force attenuation rate |
| **Control** | Local minimum → COM velocity = 0 | Control Time, LPI |

**Impulse-momentum lens:** Landing impulse must equal propulsive impulse — it is the return leg of the same transaction. A **Libero** or **Small Forward** with high Loading Rate and long Control Time is absorbing impact inefficiently: high injury risk, poor repeat-jump capacity.
**Bilateral split:** Asymmetries > 10–15% flagged immediately. Return-to-play clearance gated here.

## 04 — Strategic Insight for Coaching Staff

### The Forensic Finding

Most performance programs are optimizing the wrong variable.

Peak GRF is visible, dramatic, and easy to report. But it is a **snapshot of one moment** — and that moment can be high even when the athlete is mechanically broken upstream and downstream.

**Impulse is the integral. Force is the derivative.** Coaching staff should be reading the area under the curve, not the peak.

### The Action Threshold

Three questions for every CMJ test session:

1. **Is the Transfer Point shortening over time?** → Elastic energy recycling is improving.
2. **Is the Braking:Propulsive Impulse ratio trending toward balance?** → Eccentric-to-concentric efficiency is building.
3. **Is the bilateral Landing split below 15%?** → Return-to-play and injury risk are manageable.

If all three trend positively, the athlete is building durable speed — not just gym-floor performance.

## 05 — Command Principle: Authority and Responsibility

> **Authority and responsibility must be congruent and commensurate.**

This is the operating rule of the N1 Command Staff model. It has one implication: whoever owns the outcome must also hold the decision rights. The two cannot be separated.

### Why It Matters in This System

The CMJ data generates a prescription signal. That signal is only as good as the person acting on it — and that person can only act well if their authority matches their accountability.

**Three failure modes when they are out of alignment:**

1. **Responsibility without authority** — A strength coach is accountable for athlete readiness but cannot override a head coach's training load decision. The data becomes decoration. Outcomes degrade without a traceable cause.

2. **Authority without responsibility** — A decision-maker can prescribe, modify, or override but bears no consequence for the outcome. Diagnostic rigor collapses. The forensic system loses its signal-to-noise ratio.

3. **Diffused ownership** — Multiple staff members share access to the data but no single person owns the decision. Accountability disperses. Nothing changes until something breaks.

### Applied to the N1 Workflow

| Decision | Authority Must Rest With | Accountability Owned By |
|---|---|---|
| Training prescription (phase-specific) | S&C Coach / Lead Analyst | S&C Coach / Lead Analyst |
| Return-to-play clearance | Medical + S&C jointly | The person who signs the clearance |
| Load modification (in-season) | S&C Coach with head coach visibility | S&C Coach |
| Bilateral asymmetry flag (>15%) | S&C Coach — unilateral hold authority | S&C Coach |
| Data interpretation and report | N1 Analyst | N1 Analyst |

### The Congruence Test

Before any staff review, confirm: *Can the person presenting the data also act on it?*

If yes — the system is aligned. The forensic findings will move to action.
If no — identify where the authority gap sits and close it before the session. A data review without decision rights is a performance theatre.
