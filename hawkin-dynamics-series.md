# Force Plate Data Series · Hawkin Dynamics

## The 3 RFD Metrics in Your Hawkin Report.

---

## 01 · BRAKING RFD.

### The eccentric metric everyone tracks. And the one with the most noise.

Calculated from the steepest instantaneous slope of the force-time curve during the **braking phase** — the period where the athlete decelerates their centre of mass from peak negative velocity back to zero. The timing of this peak shifts with individual movement strategy, adding a confounding variable on top of an already noisy signal.

| APPLICATION | EXPLANATION |
|---|---|
| Braking RFD from CMJ testing carries a CV of ~15%. A measured change needs to exceed this range before you treat it as real. Use it for directional tracking across large training blocks — not for week-to-week decisions. | The timing of peak braking RFD is not fixed. Athletes who drop quickly hit it early in the phase; athletes with a slower descent hit it later. You may not be measuring the same moment between sessions or between athletes. |

---

## 02 · CONCENTRIC IMPULSE @ 100ms.

### A time-locked measure of early propulsive force production.

The fixed 100ms window removes the timing variability that undermines braking RFD. It measures the same moment in the **propulsive phase** regardless of how the athlete got there — regardless of unweighting strategy, braking depth, or countermovement speed. This is impulse, not RFD: it captures how much force the athlete applies in a fixed time window, which is closer to what actually determines jump height.

| APPLICATION | EXPLANATION |
|---|---|
| CV sits between 5 and 8% from CMJ testing — roughly twice as reliable as braking RFD. Sensitive to neuromuscular fatigue and acute training adaptations. A drop in this metric before jump height falls is an early warning signal worth tracking. | Peak RFD asks: *how fast does force rise?* Concentric Impulse @ 100ms asks: *how much force do you produce in the first moment of propulsion?* The second question has a more stable answer. The 100ms window begins at propulsion onset, so you always measure the same moment. |

---

## 03 · mRSI (MODIFIED REACTIVE STRENGTH INDEX).

### The efficiency composite. Jump height means less without the time it took to get there.

**mRSI = Jump Height ÷ Contraction Time.** It captures the full movement — eccentric loading, the transition at the bottom, propulsion — and collapses it into a single number. An athlete who reaches the same jump height in less time scores higher. An athlete who preserves height but slows down scores lower. This asymmetry is the point.

| APPLICATION | EXPLANATION |
|---|---|
| CV of ~7–10%. One of the most sensitive CMJ metrics to fatigue, readiness, and short-term training adaptations. A reliable go-to for daily or weekly monitoring without needing to interpret the full force-time curve. | A fatigued athlete often maintains jump height by extending contraction time — they compensate. mRSI catches the compensation because it penalises the extra time. Jump height alone misses it. This is why tracking height and mRSI together tells a more complete story than either one alone. |

---

## The Way You Measure Rapid Force Capacity Changes What It Means.

| # | Metric | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **BRAKING RFD** | High noise. Eccentric braking capacity. Timing varies with movement strategy. |
| **2** | **CONCENTRIC IMPULSE @ 100ms** | More stable. Early propulsive force in a fixed time window. |
| **3** | **mRSI** | The efficiency composite. Jump height relative to how long it took. |

**Methods matter before interpretation. The metric you choose shapes the question you're actually asking.**
